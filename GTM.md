# GTM.md (Go-To-Market Strategy)

## The "Impulse Buy" Problem
Whittle isn't built for procurement departments at Fortune 500 companies. It’s built for **Engineering Managers, Technical Founders, and DevOps leads** at 5–50 person startups.

In the current market, these users are "impulse buying" every new AI model and code assistant that hits Product Hunt. They start with ChatGPT Plus, then add GitHub Copilot, then the team hears Claude is better for coding so they buy Claude Max, then they realize Cursor is the future so they buy 20 Cursor seats—all while the original Copilot licenses are still active.

Whittle exists for that exact "Wait, what are we actually paying for?" moment.

---

# Where the Users Are
We don't need a massive marketing budget. We just need to be where the AI stack debates are happening.

### 1. The Comparison Culture (X/Twitter & Reddit)
Technical founders love comparing stacks. I’d target threads where people are debating:
- "Cursor vs. GitHub Copilot"
- "Claude 3.5 Sonnet vs. GPT-4o for engineering"
- "Is Gemini AI Pro worth it for workspace?"

The strategy is simple: **Manual Audit Replies.** Instead of an ad, I’d drop a link to a founder's specific problem: *"I built a tool that actually calculates the overlap between those three. Ran your stack through it—it’s usually a 30% waste."*

### 2. The "Roast My Stack" Growth Loop
People love sharing their setups. Whittle's **Public Report URLs** were built specifically for this. 
- A founder runs an audit.
- They see a "42% Waste" score.
- They share the link on X or LinkedIn with: *"Whittle just told me I'm wasting $1,200/yr on AI tools I don't use. Roast my stack."*

The Open Graph (OG) previews we implemented ensure that when that link is shared, it shows the potential savings immediately, pulling more founders into the funnel.

# How We Get the First 100 Users ($0 Budget)

### Phase 1: Contextual Hunting
I wouldn't run a single ad. I would spend 2 hours a day on **r/startups, r/SaaS, and the AI Engineer Discord** finding people complaining about their OpenAI or Anthropic bills. 
- **Action**: Provide a manual audit result as a comment. 
- **Goal**: Get the first 20 "Aha!" moments.

### Phase 2: Engineering Transparency
Most procurement tools feel like "black boxes." Whittle feels like an **engineering tool.** 
Because we built a **deterministic engine** rather than a "vague AI chat," I can actually show people the math. 
- **Action**: Post a "How the Engine Works" thread on X.
- **Goal**: Build trust with the technical users who hate "AI hype" but love clear, rule-based optimization.

### Phase 3: The "Consultation" Bridge
We’ve already implemented a **consultation lead-capture form** for users who save over a certain threshold. 
- **Action**: For every audit that saves >$500/mo, I’d offer a 15-minute "SaaS Rightsizing" call.
- **Goal**: Convert the free audit into a high-trust relationship.

# Why Whittle is Special
Most "spend management" startups try to be everything for everyone. Whittle is **opinionated.** It knows that an engineer using Cursor probably doesn't need Copilot. It knows that a 3-person team doesn't need ChatGPT Enterprise.

By focusing on **AI Native stacks**, we are entering a niche that is currently exploding but has zero procurement oversight. 

### What Success Looks Like in Week 1:
- **80+ Completed Audits**: High engagement with the multi-step form.
- **25+ Shared Links**: Seeing our OG cards in the wild on X or Slack.
- **10+ Lead Submissions**: Real founders asking for help via the consultation CTA.

**Whittle isn't just a calculator; it's the first step in a procurement strategy for the AI-first generation.**
